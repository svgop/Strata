# Research brief 6: quant/memory density (agent, 2026-10-15) — 10 findings

1. **QTIP trellis-coded quant** (arXiv:2406.11235 NeurIPS'24; Together blog; llama.cpp #10125) — TCQ beats VQ codebooks: 2-bit MSE 0.071 vs QuIP# 0.089 vs optimum 0.063; minimal degradation to 2-3 bits WITHOUT recovery fine-tuning; decode kernels match QuIP# speed. Rotated TCQ at 3.1-3.3 bpw should BEAT IQ3_S (3.44 bpw) quality at ~7-10% smaller → more experts per GiB AND better quality. Needs TCQ decode (bitshift trellis) ported into our MMQ kernel. HIGHEST-LEVERAGE weight-format change.
2. **IQ3_KS/IQ3_K in mainline llama.cpp** (merge PR #19726, Feb 2026, from ik_llama.cpp IQK family) — 3-7% better perplexity than Q3_K_M at same size; IQ2_K (~2.5 bpw) = natural cold-expert tier (−28% size vs IQ3_S). LOWEST-EFFORT A/B: quantize experts to IQ3_KS and run the KL harness.
3. **Kitty 2-bit KV** (MLSys 2026 oral; github.com/Summer-Summer/Kitty) — channel-sensitivity-ranked key cache (small 4-bit fraction + rest 2-bit as two unified tensors), page-centric layout + page-dequant Triton kernels, ~8× KV reduction near-zero loss, 2.1-4.1× throughput at same memory; TESTED ON QWEN3. 12KB/token → ~1.5-2KB; 262k-context KV 3.1GB → ~0.5GB. Page-centric = aligned with our page-4 attention. STRONGEST KV ITEM.
4. **TurboQuant TQ3 KV** (llama.cpp #20969; arXiv:2504.19874 ICLR'26) — Hadamard + 3-bit Lloyd-Max (3.25 bpw, 4.9×); K-cache "EXCELLENT" but community REFRACT tests show V-cache GENERATION-TRAJECTORY COLLAPSE (Qwen3-32B 9.97 vs 81.88 GTM) and 17-39% decode slowdown at long context on a 5090 fork. K-only at most; Kitty likely dominates.
5. **QuantMoE-Bench allocation recipe** (arXiv:2406.08155) — uniform 2-bit experts catastrophic (Mixtral 75.3→40.5), 4-bit near-lossless; ATTENTION bits beat expert bits at equal bpw (+5%); shared experts + FIRST MoE blocks deserve high bits; frequency-based boosting weak for balanced routing; Pareto ~3.29 bits. CORRECTS our tiering: rank hot experts by outlier-score+frequency, not frequency alone.
6. **GEMQ** (arXiv:2605.23078) — Fisher-sensitivity + binary-LP global bit allocation; Mixtral ~2.5 bits/expert ≈ 65.13% (vs 64.34 layer-wise); 6.1× memory cut, 1.6× speedup. Router fine-tune doesn't transfer to frozen serving; the LP math does.
7. **Unsloth Dynamic UD tier map** (unsloth.ai R1 1.58-bit; Dynamic 3.0) — production proof that sub-2-bit expert bulk survives when attention/shared/first/last stay Q4-Q8. Cold RAM-pinned tier at ~2 bpw = −42% RAM → 1.7× cheaper PCIe refills or larger cold pool.
8. **Exact two-phase lm-head: NOBODY SHIPS IT — white space confirmed** — all 2024-2026 subset methods approximate/speculative-side. The exact construction: per-token norms precomputed (248k fp16 ≈ 0.5MB), phase-0 scores a cheap q2/q4 sketch, phase-1 rescores only tokens whose Cauchy-Schwarz bound ‖h‖·‖wᵢ‖ beats the sketch's best, full Q6_K read only within bound-noise (rare). Provable exactness per step. 521MB/window → ~30-70MB; the head line becomes 0.5-2ms. BUILD IT (= our T1.8, now with a concrete exact algorithm).
9. **2:4 structured sparsity for hc/router dense projections** (CUTLASS Blackwell docs; ~1.27× GEMM real-world; metadata halving approaches 2× on reads) — sm_120 retains sparse tensor cores; prune per-projection (SparseGPT/MaskLLM one-shot). For the hc line specifically: 157MB → ~78MB/window. Skip for experts (TCQ/IQK win).
10. **Caution: "ITQ3_S" (arXiv:2603.27914) is NOT credible** — fabricated-looking specs, no code; do not cite/build. Its loader-fused-FWHT idea is still the right kernel shape for #1.
