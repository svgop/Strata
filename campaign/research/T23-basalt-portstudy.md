## Executive summary

**The number, and whether it is comparable.** The 324.6 tok/s claim is `docs/unified-kernel.md:32`: **6.675 ms/decode window, 324.6 tok/s, "separate kernels" build** — measured on the IQ3_S pack, `--peer-exclusive` (5060 Ti holds experts the 5090 does not; `src/program/generate.cpp:818,1928`), the **prose bench fixture** (chat-templated "Write an essay on the Odyssey.", a **short prompt**, 1200 tokens out, temp 0.6 — `src/program/bench_main.cpp:400,594`), **spec 7**, GPU at 400 W, labeled **"agent development A/Bs"** (single-session, their own CONTRIBUTING forbids cross-session comparison). At 149.8 windows/s that is **2.17 accepted tokens/window**.

**Verdict: NOT directly comparable as a headline — but tightly calibratable, and the calibration says the gap is real and portable.** Our probe-lane M1 (2000-token generation, 131k ctx config, five-boot fresh-boot means ~212.5; scoreboard band 203.9–212.5 across windows) is the same *class* of measurement (single-stream speculative decode throughput), differing in: spec depth (7 vs 6), tier layout (peer GPU vs our Zen5 pinned-RAM pool), session discipline (single-session A/B, ±3–5% by their own admission, vs five-boot means), and effective context (short vs 131k config). The decisive anchor is **their own**: the 8-bit-dense arm of their dense-format A/B (`docs/precision/features.md:26-32`, RTX 5090 @ 400 W, 5060 Ti peer, ABBA, 6 fresh processes/arm) ran the **same prose fixture at 213.4 tok/s** — statistically indistinguishable from our 212.5. Their 8-bit-dense config is our config (our IQ3_S pack's dense projections are **type 8 = Q8_0** — `/home/svg/LLM/data/packs/iq3_s/index.txt`; their 8-bit arm differs from us only in rig details that are common to both their arms and therefore cancel). Conclusion: **the engines were at parity on that fixture class before their Oct 8–20 kernel wave; the whole ~1.5× (their 6.675 ms vs our ~11.5 ms window; per-token 3.07 vs 4.66 ms ≈ 1.52×) accrued in named, portable commits.** Per-window (not per-token) is where they win: their accepted tokens/window (2.17) is *lower* than ours (~2.47) — drafter quality is not their advantage; window engineering is.

**What the wave contains** (all default-on, "one fast path", no env knobs — CONTRIBUTING): `dense_tc` int8-tensor-core dense projections; int8 hyper-connection copies read by one persistent kernel per hc read (`hc_fused`/`hc8_gr`); `mid_chain` persistent GDN-middle kernel + commit finisher; the PDL chain with in-kernel early triggers; the two-stage Q6_K verify head; the device-planned MoE tail extended to all expert formats incl. NVFP4; PDL-chained device-conditional drafting with a cumulative keep rule; `.basalt` single-file packs. Our tree already beats theirs on the spec-arbitration line (`chain()` lookup+MTP chaining and `propose_after` exist only on our side) and matches their device-plan MoE skeleton (shared parent).

**Ranked answer in one line:** PORT NOW = dense_tc (+Q6_K dense re-quant) and int8 hc copies — together priced at our two biggest recoverable lines (dense/gemv 3.3 ms, hc 2.87 ms); PORT WITH WORK = PDL early-trigger chain, mid_chain+finisher, two-stage verify head, device-conditional drafting + cumulative keep rule; TAKE THE IDEA = their measurement/quality-gate methodology, pack-embedded placement profiles, `.basalt` layout, (maybe) same-GPU L2 warm-up; NOT APPLICABLE = peer-tier mechanics, Windows seams, NVFP4 lane (unless we adopt NVIDIA's checkpoint), their suffix drafter (we are ahead). Dead-end scorecard: **PDL = partial reversal (they made it pay; HOW below); persistent kernel = split verdict (GDN-middle landed bitwise, whole-layer retired slower — they re-proved our R1 skepticism with better instruments); tree verify = still dead on their side too; CLC = absent from their tree entirely.**

## Per-candidate notes (file:line on both trees)

### 1. dense_tc — int8 tensor-core dense projections (PORT NOW)
- Their side: `src/kernels/cuda/dense_tc.cu:1-13` (persistent tile kernel: 16-row m-tiles, chunk ring, cp.async, first chunks issued before the PDL wait — "weights are constant"); supported types Q4_K/Q5_K/Q6_K/IQ4_XS/IQ3_S/Q3_K/Q2_0/Q8_0 (`include/basalt/kernels/dense_tc.hpp:2-12`); numerics: exact int32 per 32-block on mma, fixed-order fp32 scale sum; **not bitwise vs mmvq at Q8_0** (both ~1e-7 RMS vs float64 — precision/index.md, `dense_tc.cu:1063-1075`); commit 6e53eee ("Q8_0 dense projections take dense_tc"), 6ac1ca8 (`--requant-dense-q6k` pack rewriter — serves our queued re-quant directly).
- Their evidence: features.md:26-34 table (+7.8/+5.6/+10.2%, windows 10.00→9.24 ms), quality gate Q6_K-dense KLD +0.0063 paired (disclosed, outside noise).
- Our side: dense/gemv/attn/rec line 3.3 ms (T13A anatomy, `campaign/research/T13A-decode-critical-path.md:13`); dispatch in `src/core/verify.cpp` projection section; our mmvq path `src/kernels/cuda/native_mmvq.cu` (T19 mmvq_il adoptions stay for non-TC types and the head).
- Price: −0.3..−0.7 ms/window on Q8_0 alone; the Q6_K re-quant adds −22% dense bytes on top (their "why it is faster": 654 of 2,948 MiB). Their 8-bit arm ≈ our 212.5 is the reason this ranks #1: it is the measured delta between us and them on an otherwise-identical config.

### 2. int8 hc copies — `hc8_gr` / `hc_fused` (PORT NOW, staged)
- Their side: `src/kernels/cuda/hc8_gr.cu:1-13` — three kernels (norm/down/up) on int8 copies, "for every caller that does not run hc_fused_read: fused_gr_read, fused_gr_read_multi and gr_read hand such matrices here" (our exact entry-point names); `src/kernels/cuda/hc_fused.cu` — the whole read as ONE persistent kernel, item queue in global memory, up via fp16-split mma (`hc_fused.cu:635-698`), int8 copy build `:1173-1232`, read at `verify.cpp:1162,1428,1885,2179`; copy format: int8 codes, one fp16 scale per 32 inputs, clamped ±127 (index.md; `hc_fused.cu:343`).
- Evidence: default path of every published quality number (IQ3_S KLD 0.15264 vs llama.cpp 0.1526 — i.e., llama.cpp-parity **with** hc8 on).
- Our side: the hc line is our #1 kernel target — 2.87 ms/window, unpack8 ALU/cvt-bound (`gr_read_group` verify.cpp:1497; kernels `src/kernels/cuda/fused_gr.cu`; our norm_fast S3 flip lives at fused_gr.cu:1085-1103 — same file, staging conflict, not a logical one: norm_fast is the norm split, hc8 replaces the weight format). T17D priced the TC-hc class at +9..17% M1; hc8 is the cheap on-ramp to the same line (kills unpack + halves bytes) with the persistent one-kernel form as the stretch goal.
- Gates: bits change (documented in their precision doc as such) → our coupled-MMLU + mimic floor, not M3b.

### 3. PDL chain (PORT WITH WORK) — the F-191 reversal, and HOW they made it work
- Our closure: F-191 armed the launch attribute and measured a no-op; T17D docs-confirmed the semantics (`cudaGridDependencySynchronize` blocks until primaries are "completed and flushed"; overlap = preamble + launch latency only) and closed the lane (`campaign/research/T17D-sm120-kernel-frontier.md:66,198,221-226`). kPdlPrefetch (preamble-only) exists in our tree.
- Their mechanism — the missing half: (a) `include/basalt/kernels/pdl.hpp`: `launch_pdl` wrapper (cudaLaunchKernelEx + `cudaLaunchAttributeProgrammaticStreamSerialization`, per-kernel/per-device capability cache, `PdlScope(on, first_plain)` for boundary launches); (b) **in-kernel `pdl_launch_dependents()` early triggers at named points** — 11 kernel files, e.g. dense_tc ×5, qsa_dec_attn ×4, qsa_select ×3, native_mmvq ×2, hc_fused, mid_chain, dec_gdn_rec, shared_expert, native_moe, iq_kernels, qsa_dec_fused; history shows the iteration: 13b1a0c (QSA chain bits, default off) → b7cb16b (hc3_early: "combine triggers its dependents **at its start**") → 0212b8d/154b9b3 (early triggers as the only path) → ca533a5 (draft chain under PDL: "a Q8_0 one-column kernel loads its weights **before the wait**, the attention hc read lets the query projection in, the mixer read lets the head in with its first rows prefetched to L2") → 36d6f22 (**always on**, 2026-10-09).
- Why our no-op and their win are consistent: the attribute alone = dependent starts at primary completion (our measurement); the win comes from kernels that call launch_dependents mid-execution + dependents that stage weight reads (constant data — the sanctioned preamble) before `pdl_wait()`. Their window has far more, smaller launches (~72 PDL sites), so launch-tail tax is a bigger share than ours.
- Risk surface: issue #16's `dec_commit_finisher` failure is literally a PDL-class begun-word poll timing out on one box — this lane needs our canary/stress class (F-223) plus nsys proof of actual overlap.
- Price on our stack: T13A #9's −0.5..1.5 ms/window band stands as the ceiling; no isolated number exists on their side (deliberately folded). Graph capture: attributes survive capture; verify on our graph path first.

### 4. mid_chain + commit finisher (PORT WITH WORK, XL)
- Their side: `include/basalt/kernels/mid_chain.hpp` (kMidChainMaxT=4; one CTA per SM; phases hand over via global-memory counters; must be a programmatic dependent of the first hc read and the parent of the second), `src/kernels/cuda/mid_chain.cu` (553 lines; TMA `CUtensorMap` for rec state, mbarrier pair ring with even slot count, per-phase stamps buffer), `src/kernels/cuda/attn_chain_kernel.cuh` (788) + `attn_chain_bound.cuh` (every wait bounded by `%%globaltimer`, traps instead of hanging — a pattern we should copy for any device-side spin). Types Q4_K/Q5_K/Q6_K/IQ3_S/IQ4_XS; windows 1–4 rows; QSA layers, T≥5, batched windows keep separate kernels; **bitwise** vs the three kernels it replaces (doc + mid_chain_parity). Finisher: GDN commit claims items from a counter on its own stream; next window opens with a finisher draining the rest (`verify_kernels.cu`; commits 481be49, db90250) — our commit is already async off the critical path (verify.cpp:2708-2711), so the finisher is a minor.
- The retired full-layer kernel (unified-kernel.md:24-46) is the free lesson that validates our R1/T17D skepticism: bitwise but slower (7.541 vs 6.675 ms); experts half bound by cross-CTA dependency latency (58 µs vs 30 µs byte floor at T=3); gate/up warps stalled on **instruction fetch** (1.5 MB SASS); standalone harness predicted gains the engine didn't show (ratio 0.1–0.3) because it started CTAs with experts already known. Their "Lessons" block (register spills via cuobjdump, no split-compile — it cost 9%, silent smem fallback, width-mix conversion constant ~0.73×36/layer-µs) is methodology gold.
- Price for us: our window is node-latency-bound (5.6 µs/node, ~2,050 nodes — T13A); folding the GDN middle deletes graph nodes on 36 layers. Band +8..15% M1 if the PDL coupling comes along; without it, less. XL effort is real: TMA descriptors, residency guarantee inside our CUDA-graph launch, the pair-ring protocol.
- Caveat: the only end-to-end number spanning its landing (issue #17, third-party 16 GB box, 493dcfca→fb0944c7, 41 commits) shows decode +6..24% and fused-path prefill +41.5% — supportive, not isolating. No isolated mid_chain µs published; the in-tree microbench (`mid_chain_parity`, hot/cold) would have to be run on our card.
