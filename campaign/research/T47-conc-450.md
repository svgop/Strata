## HEADLINES

1. **450 is NOT a window problem; it is a ROUND-WALL problem.** At 9-wide ternary the engine commits
   ≈ 9 × 1.77 ≈ **15.9 tok/round** [M: T37 C3 arithmetic + M3b 76-78% class]. 450 tok/s ⇔ 28.3
   rounds/s ⇔ **35.3 ms/round end-to-end**. A normal-window 325 [H: F-286's own projection] ⇔ 48.9
   ms/round — while the per-window GPU span is 22-28 ms [M: F-286 WSTAT, T39 §1.2 cohort WSTAT p50
   23.8]. **~40-50% of the round wall is NOT the verify window** — it is the three finish tails, the
   admission stops, the adapt join, ramp/drain asymmetry, and host-feed stalls. The levers below are
   ranked by how much of that non-window share they delete; the in-window span itself is nearly
   free (three 6-row windows overlap GPU-side by construction).
2. **The two levers that can carry 325 → 400-460 are both already specified in-tree literature:**
   T37's **C2** (admission/tail overlap into the reactor — the F-249-named top conc lever) and T37's
   **C4** (a SECOND draft row on the batch lane, which at ternary wants kVerifyMaxT 8→9). The field
   (SGLang/vLLM/TRT-LLM one-step-ahead overlap; Sarathi-Serve stall-free admission) is unanimous
   that host work must hide under device work at exactly our window lengths (10-13 ms). The C4 lever
   is corroborated at our operating point by 2025-26 EAGLE/MTP serving data: spec gains GROW with
   concurrency when the drafter rides the target's weights (Parasail, P-EAGLE, EAGLE-4) — and our
   drafter is structurally on that side (T37 S4/S10).
3. **The window tax is a HOST-PLACEMENT problem with a named fix and a falsified cheap version.**
   Tonight's −10-15% (279-298 vs 320-330) lives in feed-thread preemption by chrome/agent sessions
   [M: F-286 WSTAT forensics]. The naive whole-process taskset 0-7 is FALSIFIED (−20%) [M: F-286];
   the queued fix is the F-201 CCD split + IRQ steering + unprivileged renice/SCHED_BATCH of the
   ambient burn. External grounding (eunomia): a contended host costs 8.8-20.5%, and
   taskset+nice-style controls recover ~+7.6% **[S]**. This lever banks the RECORD; it does not
   reach 450 by itself on a quiet box (our own R7 verdict: near-zero mean gain when quiet [M]).
4. **External ceiling check: the card has headroom, but only at a different operating point.**
   CloudRift's vLLM + AWQ Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B on one 5090: **1,157 tok/s aggregate at concurrency
   16** (~72 tok/s per user, TTFT ~0.96 s, NO speculative decoding) **[F]**. Our 450-at-9 goal =
   50 tok/s per user — BELOW their saturated per-user rate — so the goal is hardware-plausible;
   our deficit vs their point is our heavier per-window fixed cost (48 layers) bought back by MTP.
   The llama.cpp-class comparator (arXiv 2512.23029, Q6_K_XL on a 5090) **collapses** from 192
   tok/s single to ~47 tok/s/user at 4 users (~188 aggregate) **[F]** — no published stock stack
   serves 9 clients at 50 tok/s/user on this card class. Nobody has published our operating point.

## §2 EXTERNAL SWEEP (confidence-graded; primary source per claim)

**S1. One-step-ahead overlap is the field's default and its contract is zero-sync. [F, carried T37]**
SGLang v0.4 overlap scheduler (one batch ahead, Nsight-verified five decode batches with no GPU
idle), SGLang Spec-V2 + DFlash (+33% at conc-32 on B200; host `pop_and_process` of batch N−1 under
GPU batch N), vLLM V1 `async_scheduling` (default-ON v0.14), TRT-LLM overlap scheduler ("GPU work
for step n+1 launches without waiting for step n's CPU tasks"; cost: one extra step of latency;
**Contract: zero CPU-GPU syncs inside the overlapped region**). All [F/carried-T37-S7-S9]. T47 adds
nothing new here except the confirmation that at OUR window lengths (10-13 ms) the overlap is where
the gains concentrate (SGLang: "gains largest on small models / high TP" = host work is a large
fraction of a short step — our exact regime).

**S2. Sarathi-Serve (OSDI'24, arXiv 2403.02310) — stall-free admission is decode-FIRST. [F-abstract/S]**
Chunked-prefill batches constructed by admitting decodes first, then partially-complete prefills,
then new prefills — decodes never pause; 5.6-9.4× throughput at near-peak capacity on one GPU.
Maps to C2b: our decode-share (0.5 default) is a coarser version of their interleave; their result
says the decode share can go MUCH higher before decode feels the read — the zero-code falsifier is
`STRATA_BATCH_DECODE_SHARE=0.9` (T37's pricing boot, still never run).

**S3. Micro-batch overlap on ONE device is the consensus shape — and SM contention is the tax. [S]**
SGLang TBO (two-batch overlap: compute of micro-batch A hides all-to-all of B; shipped for
DeepSeek), LMSYS SBO (single-batch overlap), vLLM DBO for Model Runner V2 (issue #50738). TokenWeave
(arXiv 2505.11329, MLSys'26 oral): token-level overlap of two concurrent CUDA streams, up to 1.28×,
and its own caveat — **reserving SMs for the overlaid kernels slows the compute stream and eats
overlap gains**; overlap pays only where the hidden work is NOT SM-saturating. Our three cohort
windows are memory-bound weight streams co-running on separate streams/graphs — on TokenWeave's
good side, which is why F-233's two-window overlap measured clean (+24.8%). The ternary's third
window is the same class. The lesson constrains what we can hide under the windows: doorbell/expert
DMA and host tails yes; new compute kernels no.

**S4. PDL on sm_120: supported, same-stream only, works inside graphs. [F/S]**
CUDA Programming Guide PDL chapter [F]: primary triggers `cudaTriggerProgrammaticLaunchCompletion`,
secondary overlaps its preamble via `cudaGridDependencySynchronize`; CUTLASS docs: Hopper+Blackwell
[F]; llama.cpp issue #15479: "PDL is available on Hopper, Blackwell" and helps even without
overlapping kernel logic (pure launch-overhead hiding) [S]; FlashInfer ships `enable_pdl` on FA2 +
CUDA-core decode paths [S]; Triton exposes gdc_launch_dependents/gdc_wait [S]. Within graphs, kernel
nodes take the programmatic port attribute (cudaGraphKernelNodeSetAttribute). **PDL is SAME-STREAM**:
it does NOT create cross-graph overlap — the ternary's cross-cohort overlap already comes from three
independent streams + graphs, and no PDL form merges them (do not burn time looking for one).
Value is intra-window: ~1.5-6 µs node spacing (our F-201 measured 9.5 µs) compressed on ~1100-1200
nodes → the banked launch-map-diet + PDL band (+1-3% conc).

**S5. Multi-stream/multi-graph pitfalls — the enumerate list for a third verifier. [F/S]**
(a) Graphs sharing one memory pool MUST serialize (allocator aliasing; PyTorch CUDAGraph-trees
docs) [S] — our verifiers each own pools/graphs/arenas by construction (F-233), and the ternary
clone must keep that separation or the windows silently time-slice. (b) Capture-stream choice is
irrelevant at replay (execution follows the graph DAG) [S/NVIDIA forum] — irrelevant to us (we
launch pre-captured exec graphs). (c) **Sync D2H / host-function launches hold the driver lock and
block ALL streams** — our own dma-refusal law (expert_source.cpp:4776 → verify.cpp:3166-3192) is
corroborated exactly by vLLM issue #22754 (one sampled-token D2H blocked other streams' KV
transfers) [S]. The ternary tails must keep zero driver-locking calls inside the overlapped region.
(d) Lazy capture under in-flight flight = the F-233 hang class; F-284 found the stage-order law
extends to three cohorts (C stages before any go). (e) CUDA-graph conditional-node device-launch
limits (120/255/1) do NOT bind host-launched graphs (T37 S11, carried).

**S6. Host-feed isolation on dual-CCD X3D. [S + M]**
eunomia "When Does CPU Scheduling Help AI Inference?" (gthulhu/vLLM CPU-pressure study): host-side
vLLM, driver, IRQ, and NCCL work on the critical path costs **1.2% on a clean host, 8.8-20.5% under
contention; taskset+nice placement recovered +7.6% with 96% fewer context switches** [S — same
study the T35 board cites]. arXiv 2603.22774 (CPU-induced slowdowns in multi-GPU serving):
orchestration threads measurably gate inference latency [S]. Puget Systems: dual-CCD Ryzen
introduces latency effects even during GPU inference (token gen is partially CPU-bound) [S].
Placement facts on our box [M]: V-cache CCD = physical cores 0-7 (SMT siblings 16-23); nvidia
MSI-X 230/232 currently land on CPUs 19/27 (SMT siblings of workers) scattered by irqbalance;
cross-CCD ≈ 200 ns. Unprivileged levers [S/sched(7) + RHEL RT docs]: taskset/`pthread_setaffinity_np`
within the existing cpuset; `nice`+`SCHED_BATCH` on your OWN background processes (raising nice /

Negative grounding: Linux has NO X3D core-parking scheduler bias worth trusting (amd_pstate

**S7. Expert paging at high concurrency — the field's cache math says our tier is already right-sized. [S]**

**S8. Speculative decoding under load, 2025-26 scoreboard: helps when the drafter rides the target. [S]**
