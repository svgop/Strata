## 0. Summary

1. **Our compute core is now the external consensus design.** Three 2026 system papers that attack exactly our problem class — GDN-hybrid speculative verify state handling and kernels (TreeWY, arXiv 2608.20961; SpecLA, arXiv 2607.16673; ReplaySSM, arXiv 2610.13778 + NVIDIA blog) — each rebuilt, measured, and then lost against the structural choices we already run: chain-serial verify with register/SRAM-resident state, snapshots-or-replay commit off the critical path, factor buffers and trees reserved for memory-constrained concurrency. Their own numbers concede the chain case (TreeWY: trees "not yet a throughput win", chains 0.97-0.99x vs replay; SpecLA: speedup <1x at acceptance p<=0.6; ReplaySSM: "at batch size 1, no speedup"). Our banked negatives (tree-verify-dead, F-216 promote-perf-negative) are confirmed by the people who built the alternatives.
2. **The one genuinely new kernel formulation found:** MSInfer's AI-assisted GDN optimization report (arXiv 2607.16831) states the decode delta-rule output identity `o = g*(S·q) + (q·k)*delta` — the gate-prescale trick — which halves the recurrence kernel's inner-loop FMAs and is directly applicable to our `gdn_step_norm_multi_kernel` (2 FMA/elem -> 1 FMA/elem + a per-head scalar). Their NCU diagnosis (decode GDN at small batch is **instruction-latency-bound, not HBM-bound**) mechanizes the same verdict T12/T17D reached for our hc line, and their per-head-scalar trick is a cheap different-bits rung inside the dec_gdn_rec band.
3. **The biggest un-executed lever on the board is unchanged and now externally calibrated: TC-hc mma kernels** (T17D #1, +9..17% M1, effort L). The 2026 calibration arrives from the FA-5090 write-up (gau-nernst): hand-written `mma.sync` bf16/fp32-acc attention on a 5090 reaches 197.7 TFLOPS = 94.4% of the 209.5 whitepaper SOL using only Ampere-era primitives (cp.async + ldmatrix, no TMA, no warp specialization) — proof on our exact silicon that the tensor pipe is reachable from the hc read's shapes while the CUDA-core unpack path is hard-capped at 64 MACs/clk/SM (f16x2 rate inverted vs Ampere; T17D mechanized). The cheap ALU rungs on this line are now measured dead-ends (UPM_COLS +3.83% solo but flat-in-stack; staged-4-warp -6.99%; HC_REQ8 -7.17% [banked]) — the pipe swap is the remaining mechanism on the window's largest line.
4. **The spec-economics layer gained a named harness:** Nightjar (arXiv 2512.22420) formalizes exactly what F-270 discovered empirically — the optimal offered depth is a function of batch contention, and cutting rows on uncontended windows is strictly negative. Its bandit formulation `gamma*(B) = argmax g_{B,gamma}` with a switching-cost penalty is the principled policy for our per-T graph family (we already capture exec_[T] for T=1..8, so graph-shape switching is free on our engine — Nightjar's KV-reconstruction cost term is ~0 here), and it is the designated consumer of the banked T31 keep-rule refit once conditional nodes make window shapes dynamic.
5. **F-252 stands, now doubly confirmed.** No Blackwell fp32-accum unlock exists (re-searched 2026-10-23); FA-5090's measured ceiling (94% of 209.5 with fp32 accum) and cuDNN's 203.6 show even NVIDIA's own kernels treat 209.5-class as the fp32-accum roof on GB202. The sm_120 fp8/fp4 `mma.sync` block-scaled paths exist but are pack-line changes with fresh evidence of sharp edges (vLLM #51884 scale-factor transform failures on sm120; ldmatrix-fp8 operand pitfalls on the NVIDIA forums).

## 3. THE TOP-3, ARGUED IN DETAIL

### 3.1 TC-hc mma kernels — the pipe swap on the window's largest soft line
The hc read applies, per layer and per hc stream, `R_out = R + bo*down(norm(R)) + inj*up(...)`-class low-rank (320) projections over N=2560 with hc=4 streams. The weights (~26 MB/layer, bf16) are byte-floored at ~0.74 ms/window; the measured line is ~2.87 ms (T13A era), i.e. ~3.8x off the floor, because the dot is executed as an int-unpack + fp32 FMA chain whose issue path T17D mechanized at **64 MACs/clk/SM** (the consumer-Blackwell inversion: packed f16x2 runs at HALF the fp32 rate — add.f16x2 = 64 vs add.f32 = 128 results/clk/SM, CUDA 13.4 CC 12.0 table [banked]). Every cheap rung against that cap has now been measured: UPM_COLS 16->8 = +3.83% solo, FLAT in stack, default-off; the 82-block staged-down 4-warp form = -6.99% REJECTED; HC_REQ8 (byte cut) = -7.17% REJECTED because it forfeits the cp.async staged reads [all banked]. What remains is changing the ENGINE, not the schedule: `mma.sync.aligned.m16n8k16.row.col.f32.bf16.bf16.f32` with the four hc streams batched on the mma N axis so tiles fill even at T=1 (T17D's formulation), accumulating in fp32 at the F-252-correct 238 TFLOPS assumption. The 2026 calibration: gau-nernst's FA-5090 build reaches **197.7 TFLOPS = 94.4% of the 209.5 whitepaper SOL** on a 5090 with bf16 mma.sync + cp.async + ldmatrix alone (fp32 softmax/accum), and cuDNN hits 203.6 — the tensor pipe is reachable from plain-Ampere-primitive code on our silicon, no tcgen05 needed (which sm_120 lacks anyway [banked]). Formulation sketch: the hc read per stream is `y[s] = gamma * (down_s . xn_s)` + injections; batched over s=0..3 the GEMM is [4 x 2560] x [2560 x 320] per read — an m16n8 tile shape with M=16 padding of the 4-stream axis, K=2560 = 160 mma-k steps, exactly the FA-5090 v5 structure (cp.async double-buffer, ldmatrix.x4, 2-stage pipeline). Bits: fp32-accumulation reorders the sum vs the sequential dot — different bits, KL-gated (the same class GDN_CHUNKED passed at ~1e-7); the reads stay staged bf16, so the HC_REQ8 failure mode does not apply. Falsifier first: `fused_gr_bench` with an mma instantiation; adopt path: canary + KL yardstick + 3v3 + 2x MMLU (the full different-bits ladder). This is the only single code lever with a >10% band left on the board, and the 2026 evidence strengthens every premise it rests on.

### 3.2 The verify-head two-rung — the only untouched fixed-cost line
The head reads 248,320 x 2560 Q6_K = 521.5 MB **once per window regardless of T** at 0.43-0.48 ms = 63-68% of the rate the expert-hit line demonstrates on the same card — a fixed 0.45 ms tax on every window with an internal inconsistency (two other lines run at 87-92% of peak). Rung (a) is bitwise: `native_mmvq_il` is gated T>=2 (verify.cpp:1863-1867) and the sm_120 IL table ships in-tree (docs/MMVQ_IL_TABLE.md), so the T=1 slice (and any window the support check rejects) runs the plain mmvq; the T13A sizing (-0.10..0.18 ms = +2..4 t/s) has never been falsified or landed. Rung (b) is the basalt two-stage head (their features.md A/B; T24 "port with work"; original T1.6 "4-bit shortlist -> exact int8 re-score"): a cheap first pass shortlists candidates, the full-precision pass runs only the shortlist — the same 521.5 MB drops to a fraction plus a small stage-1 read. It changes bits on the main head, so it rides the quality law (2x MMLU + M3b) — exactly as basalt gated it. Arithmetic for (b): stage-2 at ~40-60K rows cuts ~0.25..0.35 ms/window => at 2.47 tok/window that is ~+4..7% M1 before composition; even half of that clears the quiet-window bar. The two rungs compose with lever 1 (both are T-flat line reductions, and T-flat savings multiply the value of every accepted row — the F-270 lesson).

### 3.3 The conditional-graph keep-rule (gamma*(B)) — converting two banked falsifications into a live lever
F-269 calibrated the cum keep-rule on the 1,615-window trace (+4.9% net model-side, positive in all 15 segments); F-270 then falsified it solo (-4.4%) and produced the law: **on fixed-shape captured graphs, never withhold draft rows on the solo lane — row-cutting pays only where row cost is real (batch contention)**. The 2026 external convergence is exact: Nightjar (arXiv 2512.22420, Feb 2026) frames offered depth as a per-batch-size decision `gamma*(B) = argmax_gamma g_{B,gamma}` with a switching-cost penalty, learns it by contextual bandit, and reports up to +14.8% throughput vs fixed-length SD under fluctuating load [sourced]. On our engine the harness is cheaper than theirs: all window widths T=1..8 are pre-captured (exec_[T]), so graph-shape switching costs ~0 and Nightjar's dominant cost term (draft-model KV reconstruction, 17.87-102 ms on their RTX 4090) does not exist; what remains is a policy over the banked per-depth acceptance curve. The missing primitive is the device-side conditional (`cudaGraphCondition`, CUDA 13): today the min_p stair is a host loop between draft-step graph launches (mtp.cpp:1697), whose naive removal F-229 measured negative (-2.73% — wasted step graphs cost more than the host bubbles they save). A conditional node inverts the trade: the chain continues in-graph iff the device-side dprob keeps the F-269 threshold, so there are no host bubbles AND no wasted steps — the two measured losses of the two naive forms cancel. Disposition unchanged from F-269/F-270: th=0 bit-identical capture first (one boot, canary only), the T31-calibrated threshold as the arm, value realized on the COHORTS/pair lanes where dm+keep already cleared the pair bar (+4.9% [banked]), and the T14B retrain deepens the chains that make both the option value and the F-229 waste term grow. vLLM names full-drafter-graph capture + dynamic speculative length its own future work (issue #33341; per-sequence K RFC) — we would be first to ship the device-conditional form on a per-T graph family.

## 4. CONTRADICTIONS WITH BANKED LAWS/NEGATIVES (challenged only with primary evidence)

1. **F-252 (fp32-accum 2:1 lock)** — NO contradiction found; CONFIRMED twice. Searched 2026-10-23 for any GB202 unlock or documented exception: none exists (gpu-unlock remains GA102/104 PCI-ID-gated [banked]; vGPU-unlock-blackwell is hardware-blocked). Independent confirmation from outside: FA-5090's own ceiling chase tops out at 197.7 TFLOPS with fp32 accumulation (94.4% of the whitepaper 209.5), cuDNN at 203.6 — nobody publishes fp32-accum numbers above the 2:1-consistent band. The mxfp8/nvfp4 `mma.sync` block-scaled paths on sm_120 (forum-documented m16n8k64 fp4 fragment layouts) are real but are pack-line changes, already priced out by T17D/T27-1, with new evidence they are sharp (vLLM #51884: DeepGEMM "Unknown SF transformation" failures on sm120, Aug 2026; fp8-ldmatrix operand pitfalls, NVIDIA forums).
2. **Megakernels-dead** — NO contradiction. FlashMoE (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2506.04667) ships a fully-fused persistent MoE megakernel — the same class basalt built, measured slower (7.541 vs 6.675 ms), and retired with root causes [banked T25-5]; FlashMoE's context is distributed training/inference with device-initiated comm, not our single-GPU decode, and its 9x claim is vs a non-fused distributed baseline [donor-reported]. Our DUMMY_NODES pricing + basalt's post-mortem stand.
3. **Tree-verify-dead** — CONFIRMED BY THE BUILDERS, with one scoped refinement. TreeWY (arXiv 2608.20961, B200, Qwen3.5 35B/397B in vLLM) is the missing tree formulation (the gated delta rule as one lower-triangular solve `(I + diag(beta)G) V' = R` over DFS-ordered nodes, 128x smaller state storage) — and its own evaluation says wider trees raise acceptance length (3.23->3.58) but are "not yet a throughput win" (piecewise capture + tokens-per-step costs), with chains trailing the replay baseline 0.97-0.99x. SpecLA (arXiv 2607.16673) ships tree-masked parallel verification and reports hybrid-verify beating root-to-leaf replay 1.80-7.11x in microbenchmarks, but its controlled end-to-end study shows net speedup <1x at acceptance p<=0.6 [both sourced]. At our standing acceptance 74-87% and T14C's 0.72 tok/row breakeven, trees remain dead for throughput. The refinement: the tree/factor machinery is a **memory** lever (TreeWY: 2-3x lower peak KV usage; SpecLA factors ~128 KiB/layer vs 3.1 MB snapshots) — relevant to T33's elastic-slot era ONLY if STRATA_GDN_SLOTS ever arms (its arena is 36 layers x T8 x 3.146 MB ~ 906 MB, verify.cpp:795-816); it is opt-in and not armed in the standing launcher.
4. **F-216 promote-perf-negative vs SpecLA's "factor buffering 3.45x faster than token replay"** — apparent contradiction, resolved by measurement context: SpecLA's replay cost (29.73 ms for 4 tokens, Triton/H100) is a per-step runtime on a state-in-HBM path; our commit replay is 36 fused CUDA kernels on an async stream that F-216 measured as ~free ("removing it entirely moves nothing" [banked]). Their factor buffering buys rollback STORAGE and recovery latency on stacks that pay for replay; ours does not pay. ReplaySSM's 1.85-2.1x [donor-reported, NVIDIA blog] likewise targets stacks that write state to HBM every decode step — our verify kernel holds state in registers and touches HBM once per window per layer. No action; recorded so nobody re-imports the class on the strength of external headlines.
5. **F-229 (ungated draft negative) vs the industry's "full CUDA graph for the drafter" (vLLM #33341)** — not a contradiction: vLLM's target is Python-dispatch bubbles we do not have (our host is C++ with ~0.1 ms bubbles); F-229's mechanism was wasted step graphs. The synthesis both measurements point to is the device-conditional form (lever 3), which removes the bubbles without the waste.

## 5. HONEST NEGATIVES (studied this round, rejected, why)

Revisit only if GDN_SLOTS arms for the pair lane or the elastic-session slot fights start binding against the arena.
- **SpecLA factor buffering + delayed state update**: same resolution as above on solo; the genuinely novel piece (delayed update fusing accepted-state fold into the NEXT verify kernel) targets a commit boundary we already overlap with the async commit graph + finisher.
- **OEA / opportunistic expert activation** (ICML 2026): -39% MoE-layer decode latency at batch 16 by re-routing tokens onto already-fetched experts — output-changing by construction (their own accuracy framing is "within standard-error"); F-230's 1.8e-4-KL acceptance sensitivity kills it for an exact-match contract. Recorded as the sharpest external negative.
- **Complementary quantization** (arXiv 2410.11305): makes DRAFT errors correlate with TARGET errors to raise acceptance — our T14B already implements the winning half (harvest trains the drafter ON the quantized engine's outputs, i.e. the draft learns the target-as-deployed). The target-side flip (quantize the target to agree with the draft) is backwards for us. Validates the harvest design; no new arm.
- **xHC / mHC hyper-connection variants** (arXiv 2607.14530; DeepSeek mHC): training-time architecture changes — inapplicable to frozen weights; corroborate only that hc stream-widening cost is a real research concern.
- **GDN chunked-parallel (WY) form at decode**: the prefill WY win (F-224, 1.68x at 32K) does not transfer to the T<=8 verify window — the serial in-kernel loop already keeps state in registers (1 read/1 write per window per layer), and WY at T<=8 adds triangular-solve overhead for zero byte savings. SpecLA's state-resident serial kernel is the same verdict from the outside.
- **QSA "online top-k" class** (fla-org NSA kernel; cuDNN CuTe-DSL DSA): our indexer scores pooled BLOCKS (max_blocks_ per window), so the score tensor is tiny and top-k is already a single launch — the NSA token-level online-selection problem we would be solving does not exist at block granularity. TC-QSA-decode (T17D #3) remains the only live attention lever.
- **sm_120 fp8/fp4 tensor pathways**: exist (m16n8k64 nvf4 mma.sync documented on the forums; FlashInfer ships block-scaled FP8 on SM120) but require a pack-line change and carry fresh sm120 scale-factor hazards (vLLM #51884); priced out twice before (T17D, T27-1), now with a third independent confirmation of the sharp edges.
- **Nightjar's elastic draft-model offload**: irrelevant — our drafter is a 90.5M dense head riding the verify graph, no VRAM competition worth a management layer.
