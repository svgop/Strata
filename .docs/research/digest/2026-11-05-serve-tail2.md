# Serve tail-2 digest (2026-11-05) — tail latency, smoothness, admission fairness at single-box scale

Question: which structural serve-layer changes most improve tail latency (p99 TTFT), smoothness (inter-chunk gaps), and admission fairness for OUR shape — one box, 1–6 concurrent streams, 262K contexts, engine busy 12.5% of wall — from production scheduler code and measured-tail literature? Companion to `2026-11-04-serving-smoothness-reliability.md` (owns Sarathi/TWP/SLO-tiering breadth); this digest goes one level deeper on scheduler code, chunk magnitude, the Python-runtime question, and instrumentation granularity, and prices every item against this tree's actual locks and queues.

## 0. Standing state verified in this tree (the facts every finding prices against)

- **Serve layer is threads, not asyncio.** `ThreadingHTTPServer` (server.py:56, `class Server` :7616), thread-per-connection; engine = separate process; one pump thread reads its stdout (`_pump`, :1250). The mandate's asyncio premise does not hold; §F5 prices the threaded equivalents.
- **Admission = `ctl` lock + `slot_cv` condition + FIFO tickets** (:1228-1247): `ctl` = "one admission or solo request on the control lines at a time"; F-14 tickets remove wake-and-race; STRATA_ADMIT_SHORT_FIRST opt-in (default off, :322-326); queue deadline → `QueueWaitExpired`; SV-8's ~113 ms is this lock's measured hold window.
- **Prefill cadence gate is in-tree and OFF standing** (:327-341, `_cadence_open` :1874-1886, the give-back-the-lines wait :2016-2040): one prompt read per `PREFILL_CADENCE_N=2` decode rounds; **waiver** when no slot is busy; tick = `BT` lines (counts tokens, not windows). Verified: no config/launcher arms `STRATA_PREFILL_CADENCE` or `STRATA_ADMIT_SHORT_FIRST`.
- **The engine owns chunk size; the server never slices prompts.** Qwen config arms `--prefill auto:32768` (config/custom-iq3_s-8081.json, verified); `PP_CHUNK_MAX=32768` (:292) sizes only the watchdog's patience (:334-336). One chunk = 32,768 tokens ≈ **5.1 s at 6.3K tok/s** — and the tree's own comment records the consequence: "a decode window that also reads a prompt chunk cannot replay the decode graph, so one 32k-token prompt read can add seconds to every decoding request's inter-token time" (:327-329).
- **Give-way exists on the solo path** (#656, `BYIELD` :1951-1969, :2123): a prompt read yields at PP chunk boundaries to a waiting request with prompt ≤ half its length, at most `YIELDS_MAX=2`.
- **SSE path**: per-event `wfile.write` + flush (:7355-7360), keep-alive comments on PP lines and every 10 s, `X-Accel-Buffering: no`, HTTP/1.0 close-ends-stream. Slot affinity exists: `pick_slot` prefers the slot holding the conversation prefix (:1996-1998).
- **Metrics**: `Latencies` = ttft/itl/e2e (vLLM buckets) + fine `gap`/`admit` histograms (:4060-4062, serve/prometheus.py T35 A6). `cadence_wait_s` exists as a debug field only (:1916). No prefill-time decomposition, no yield/cadence counters exported, no ctl-hold distribution.

## 1. Findings

### F1. Production converged on decode-first budget + prefill cadence; interval=2 is the measured sweet spot — and the win is specific to OUR regime
[PR #44558](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/44558) (fetched, merged 2026-06-16). Mechanism: on non-cadence-aligned steps defer all prefill compute (running chunks skipped, waiting prefills not admitted); decodes still fill the step. Measured (DeepSeek-R1 FP4, DP=8, cudagraph, Poisson): **P50 ITL 274.9 → 32.1 ms (−88%)**, mean TTFT −38%, **P99 TTFT 2476 → 1475 ms (−40%)**, throughput flat. Interval sweep: interval=2 best (P99 TTFT 630 ms vs 4375 at 1 and 3175 at 3) — interval=3 throttles prefill admission too hard and the backlog pushes the TTFT tail back up. Two guards matter: (a) **capacity-bound saturation guard** — when an admitting step cannot drain the waiting prefill queue, throttling is suppressed ("a saturated rank never avalanches"); (b) the win is regime-specific — on a prefill-heavy workload (16k-in, ~80:1 P:D) it gave **no ITL win and regressed TTFT**. Confidence: high (production-measured, U-shape named). Why it transfers: our box is the decode-bound, sub-saturation regime (engine busy 12.5%, 1–6 streams); our in-tree waiver (admit at once when no slot decodes) IS the saturation guard's shape.

### F2. The un-priced half of smoothness is chunk MAGNITUDE, not just frequency
[Sarathi-Serve](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02310v3) (fetched 2026-11-04, carried): per-iteration token budget from the TBT SLO; strict-SLO budget **512**, relaxed **2048**; chunking overhead ≤~25% at 512, "almost negligible" at 2048; **power-of-two alignment is load-bearing** — 257 vs 256 tokens added **32% prefill time** (tile quantization); naive hybrid batching without chunking = up to 28.3× TBT increase. [SGLang docs](https://docs.sglang.ai/advanced_features/server_arguments.html) (fetched): `--chunked-prefill-size` (−1 disables; OOM guidance 4096; `--max-prefill-tokens` default 16384), `--enable-mixed-chunk` default False. vLLM V1 scheduler source (fetched): `token_budget = max_num_scheduled_tokens`; running requests consume it first, waiting prefills get leftovers (decode-first packing, the shipped Sarathi Algorithm-3 shape); `long_prefill_token_threshold` clips one chunk — **and is waived when only one request exists** ("When it is the only request there is nobody to starve, so let it use the whole budget") — the same nobody-to-starve waiver our gate has.

At our constants: 32768-token chunk = 5.08 s; 8192 = 1.30 s; **2048 = 325 ms ≈ 20–36 decode windows**; **512 = 81 ms ≈ 5–9 windows**; 64 = 10 ms ≈ 1 window. Today's `--prefill auto:32768` makes the magnitude term unbounded relative to the 9–16 ms decode window. Confidence: high on the mechanism (three independent production stacks); the exact overhead percentage on THIS engine is unmeasured — that is R2's falsifier.

### F3. Preemption: recompute-only in production, and it is priced out at 262K — avoidance is the policy
vLLM V1 scheduler source (fetched): preemption = RECOMPUTE (`_preempt_request` frees blocks, `num_computed_tokens=0`, request prepended to waiting); victim = `running[-1]` (newest) under FCFS, or max-`(priority, arrival)` under the first-class `SchedulingPolicy.PRIORITY`; the scheduler tries to avoid preemption entirely via conservative scheduling (SGLang's sibling knob: `--schedule-conservativeness`, "raise it if you see requests being retracted frequently"). At 150–175K live contexts one preempt-recompute ≈ 24–28 s of pure re-read (prior digest §2.1 economics). Our tree already has the avoidance set: BYIELD give-way (#656), cadence spacing (P-A), nobody-to-starve waiver. Confidence: high. Named check: a preemption/yield counter (R0) should stay at ~0 BYIELDs-per-100-admissions; a rising count means admission shape is wrong, not that we need a preempt mode.

### F4. Admission fairness: FCFS is the production default; short-first-with-aging is the low-concurrency lever we already own
vLLM default policy = FCFS (SGLang docs fetched: `--schedule-policy` default fcfs); PRIORITY is opt-in for multi-tenant SLO mixes. Our `STRATA_ADMIT_SHORT_FIRST` + `ADMIT_MAX_WAIT_S=30` (:318-326, `_admit_next` :1844-1854) is coarse SRPT with an anti-starvation aging rule — the classical shape. At 1–6 streams the fairness case is exactly one: a 261K re-prefill (≈42 s of reads) against a small chat turn; short-first turns "wait behind every agent read" into "wait behind the one read in progress" (the tree's own framing, :320-321). Search-level support: TGI v3 beats vLLM on TTFT at low concurrency (up to 13× long-context with prefix caching) — at tiny concurrency, TTFT is decided by cache hits and admission shape, not batch-scheduling throughput tricks. Confidence: medium-high (mechanism in-tree and reasoned; benefit size unmeasured here).

### F5. Python runtime: the threaded server is not the tail driver — and the mandate's asyncio premise inverts into a verification result
Verified: no asyncio loop exists; uvloop is N/A. Production's answer to the reader-vs-writer structure is **process separation** (vLLM V1: API server process ↔ EngineCore process over ZMQ; SGLang: TokenizerManager ↔ Scheduler over ZMQ+msgpack, overlap scheduler runs one batch ahead) — **we already have the process split**: engine binary + a pump thread that does only line-parse + queue puts (verified :1250-1302; control telemetry never enters token streams). GIL: the switch interval is an *ideal* timeslice — "the actual value can be higher, especially if long-running internal functions or methods are used... which thread becomes scheduled at the end of the interval is the operating system's decision" ([docs.python.org](https://docs.python.org/3/library/sys.html#sys.setswitchinterval), fetched). Arithmetic at our shape: 6 streams × ~166 t/s ≈ 1,000 SSE events/s of ~1 KB `json.dumps` ⇒ well under 1% GIL share — GIL convoy is not the tail driver. The genuine Python-runtime analog of "lock-held await" is **SV-8: the ~113 ms `ctl` hold window** — a lock held across admission I/O. vLLM's pattern (schedule() never blocks on I/O; admission is metadata) maps to: hold `ctl` only across the GEN/BGEN stdin write + engine accept, release before the first wait. `slot_q`/`lines` are unbounded `queue.Queue` (:1187, :1210) — bufferbloat between engine stdout and socket is memory-only at our client count (a slow client's ITL is bound by its own TCP read; the single-decoder engine serializes rounds regardless), so bounded queues are a hygiene item, not a tail lever. Confidence: high on the inversion (verified code + arithmetic); medium on whether the 113 ms is admission-code or engine-read — R0 resolves it before anyone refactors.

### F6. Observability: production exposes per-request PHASE histograms; we are three histograms short of seeing our own tail regressions
[vLLM metrics docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/serving/metrics.html) (fetched): per-request phase decomposition — `vllm:request_queue_time_seconds`, `vllm:request_prefill_time_seconds`, `vllm:request_decode_time_seconds`, `vllm:request_inference_time_seconds`, `vllm:e2e_request_latency_seconds` + TTFT/TPOT histograms with fixed buckets + `vllm:num_preemptions_total`. [SGLang](https://docs.sglang.ai/advanced_features/server_arguments.html) (fetched): `--enable-request-time-stats-logging` (per-request time stats), configurable TTFT/ITL/e2e buckets. Ours (verified): ttft/itl/e2e/gap/admit histograms with quantile export. The gap that matters: **TTFT is not decomposed** — we cannot tell a queue/admission regression from a prefill regression per change; and the ctl-hold duration (SV-8's 113 ms) has no distribution. Confidence: high; this is the cheapest instrumentation class in the campaign.

## 2. Ranked interventions for THIS rig

Protocol note: serve layer is shared by both engines — run the cross-engine change protocol (bench DS → bench Qwen → change → bench both) on every adopt. Latency-law form: adopt on a measured tail improvement with floors held (floors here = solo 262K TTFT within +4%, prefill marginal rate ≥6K tok/s, lane banks unchanged).

### R0 — Instrument first (small serve-layer commit; co-requisite of R1/R2)
- **Change**: (a) `prefill_time` histogram — observe `first_token − admitted_at` where `latency_sink.observe_admit` fires (:1840) or at first token in the request loop; (b) `ctl_hold` histogram — observe `release − acquire` at every `self.ctl.release()` site in `generate_batched`/`_take_control` (prices SV-8's 113 ms as a distribution); (c) counters `yields_total` (BYIELD sends :1961/:2123), `cadence_waits_total` + exported `cadence_wait_s`, incidents already exist; (d) one DONE-phase log line: `queue_ms / prefill_ms / decode_ms / reused` (SGLang request-time-stats pattern).
- **Files**: serve/server.py (observe sites above) + serve/prometheus.py (two `Histogram`s + two counters; quantile export already generic).
- **Predicted**: zero perf effect; converts R1/R2/R4 from guesses into A/B-legible measurements.
- **Falsifier**: n/a (measurement); accept if /metrics grows the four series with no wall-clock change.

### R1 — SINGLE BEST FIRST CHANGE: arm `STRATA_PREFILL_CADENCE=1` standing (env-only; mechanism already in-tree)
- **Change**: add `STRATA_PREFILL_CADENCE=1` to the engine env (config `env` dict; N stays 2). No code. Verify with R0's cadence counters.
- **Mechanism (named pattern)**: vLLM PR #44558's cadence gate, shipped in-tree with the same waiver-as-saturation-guard: the gate lives in `_cadence_open` (:1874-1886), the admission waits with the control lines given back (:2016-2040), the tick counts tokens per `BT` line (:1283-1289).
- **Predicted tail effect**: mid-decode prompt-read spikes drop from every-round to one-per-2-rounds; upstream measured at interval=2: P50 ITL −88%, P99 TTFT −40%, throughput flat (F1). Our gap histogram's tail bucket population should collapse for concurrent streams.
- **Floors/falsifier**: solo TTFT must not move (waiver admits at once when nothing decodes — if solo 262K TTFT regresses >4%, the waiver failed: revert). Concurrent case: if gap p99 does not improve with ≥2 decoders + a mid-decode prefill, revert. Note the regime boundary from F1: if traffic becomes prefill-heavy (back-to-back 261K re-prefills), expect TTFT regression — that is the documented edge, not a bug.

### R2 — Shrink the engine prefill chunk: `--prefill auto:32768` → `auto:2048` (power of two), after R0 prices the per-chunk admission overhead
- **Change**: one value in config/custom-iq3_s-8081.json (+ the DS config when benched). Keep cadence armed (R1) — cadence bounds frequency, chunk size bounds magnitude; together they are Sarathi's stall-free shape.
- **Predicted tail effect**: worst single decode-gap from a prompt read drops 5.08 s → 325 ms (2048); if gap p99 still spikes, 512 → 81 ms (Sarathi strict budget; overhead ≤25% worst-case there). Power-of-two mandatory (F2's 32% tile-quantization penalty). TTFT@262K roughly unchanged *if* per-chunk admission overhead is ms-scale — R0's ctl_hold histogram decides: 128 chunks × 113 ms would be +14 s TTFT, which would instead argue for 8192 (1.30 s gaps) or a narrower ctl window (R4) first. Watchdog allowances auto-shrink with the chunks (PP-line rate formula, :2382, :2433-2434) — no separate change needed.
- **Floors/falsifier**: prefill marginal rate <6K tok/s at a lone request (bank: 6,925 fresh / 6.3K live) → reject; TTFT@262K +>4% → reject; gap p99 of concurrent decoders unimproved → reject.

### R3 — Arm `STRATA_ADMIT_SHORT_FIRST` (value ≈ a chat-turn ceiling, e.g. 4096 tokens) for mixed agent+chat traffic
- **Change**: env-only; in-tree ordering (:1772-1854) already implements SRPT-with-30 s-aging on `slot_cv`'s `wait_lens` under the F-14 ticket discipline. Run only when traffic mixes long agent reads with short turns (our actual usage).
- **Predicted tail effect**: p99 TTFT of short turns behind a 261K re-prefill drops from queue-behind-full-read (≈40 s) to one-read-behind; long prompts bounded by the 30 s aging rule (never starved).
- **Falsifier**: all-long workloads regress >4% mean TTFT, or any observed wait exceeds `ADMIT_MAX_WAIT_S` (starvation) → revert. Measure with R0's queue-time decomposition.

### R4 — Narrow the `ctl` window (SV-8) — ONLY after R0 shows the 113 ms is admission code, not engine read
- **Change (named pattern)**: metadata-only admission (vLLM's schedule() never blocks on I/O). Concretely: hold `ctl` across the GEN/BGEN stdin write + engine accept only (`wlock` already serializes stdin writes, :1246), release before the first PP wait; the give-back-while-waiting dance (:2016-2040) already proves the release/resume path is safe, including the deadlock comment (a BYIELD-held read needs the lines back — long/long/short reproduced in 0.1.41).
- **Predicted tail effect**: admission p99 (queue-time component of TTFT) shrinks; inter-chunk gaps unchanged.
- **Falsifier**: R0 shows ctl-hold p50 ≈ read time (the hold IS the read) → do not refactor; R2's chunk shrink is the correct lever instead.

## 3. What NOT to do (single-box constraint respected; no rewrites)

- **No asyncio/uvloop migration.** No event loop exists; threads are not the bottleneck at 6 clients (F5 arithmetic); production solved the reader-vs-writer structure with process separation, which this tree already has. A rewrite adds risk for zero measured tail.
- **No multi-process/microservice API layer, no distributed anything.** Queue depths of 0–2; the constraint is scale law, not defect.
- **No SWAP preemption, no recompute-based fairness.** At 262K, one recompute ≈ 24–28 s (F3); BYIELD + cadence + waiver are the avoidance set; watch the preemption counter, never build the mechanism.
- **No SSE write batching** (SGLang `--stream-interval > 1` shape). Per-event flush is the smoothness feature; batching saves microseconds of syscall and costs inter-event gaps.
- **No HTTP framework rewrite.** SV-1's 30–150 ms is harness-side re-render, not this server; HTTP/1.0 + SSE keep-alive comments are already correct for the client set.
- **No priority/preemption scheduler port** (vLLM `SchedulingPolicy.PRIORITY` targets multi-tenant SLO mixes). One operator, FIFO + short-first suffices.
- **Do not slice prompts in the server.** The engine owns chunking by design (`PP_CHUNK_MAX` sizes watchdog patience only, :334-336); server-side slicing would duplicate the C++ path and desync PP watchdog accounting.

## 4. Boundary of the conclusion

Every predicted effect is priced in serve-layer terms against measured constants (6.3K tok/s prefill, 9–16 ms windows, 113 ms ctl window). The engine-side chunking overhead at 2048 on THIS engine is unmeasured — R0's ctl-hold histogram and R2's falsifier close that before adoption. If, after R1+R2, the gap histogram's tail is still populated, the residual is the engine's own round anatomy (lane/GPU terms owned by the engine digests), not the serve layer — this digest's class is then exhausted.
