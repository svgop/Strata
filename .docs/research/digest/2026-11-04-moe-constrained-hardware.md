# MoE on constrained hardware — hybrid CPU-GPU systems digest (2026-11-04)

Framing: every system below is priced against our two walls — the DRAM ceiling (49.5 GB/s; only fewer bytes moves the lane) and the serial attn→moe chain per sequence. "Full-GPU" baselines in the literature mean the model fits in GPU VRAM; ours never does (78 GB IQ3_S vs 12 GB), so the relevant comparator for each system is its *hybrid mechanism*, not its absolute tok/s on different silicon.

## 1) KTransformers (SOSP'25, Tsinghua) — closest cousin

- **Mechanism.** MLA attention + KV + shared expert on GPU (BF16/FP8); routed experts in RAM on CPU with Intel AMX INT4/INT8 kernels (v0.3 online-quantizes BF16→INT8 at load). NUMA: critical matrices duplicated per socket; `numactl` pinning; `--cpu_infer` just under core count. [tutorial doc](https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers/blob/main/doc/en/DeepseekR1_V3_tutorial.md), [repo](https://github.com/kvcache-ai/ktransformers). Overlap: **asynchronous CPU-GPU task scheduling over "continuous CUDA graphs"** — hybrid CPU+GPU execution captured so CPU expert compute and transfers do not break the graph; launch overhead cut "from over 20% to nearly zero" ([SGLang blog](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2025-10-22-KTransformers); SOSP PDF [Tsinghua](https://madsys.cs.tsinghua.edu.cn/publication/ktransformers-unleashing-the-full-potential-of-cpu/gpu-hybrid-inference-for-moe-models/SOSP25-chen.pdf) — fetch timed out, mechanism cross-confirmed via SGLang blog + [noze.io analysis](https://www.noze.io/en/insights/ktransformers-hybrid-cpu-gpu-inference)). "CSDI" as named in our task: no such term found in repo/docs/paper — the real mechanism is the async-scheduler-over-CUDA-graphs above (searched "CSDI" + aliases; [search](https://www.google.com/search?q=KTransformers+CSDI) surfaced nothing; treat "CSDI" as a stale/unverifiable label).
- **Measured.** Xeon Gold 6454S 2×32C + 4090D 24GB, DS-V3 Q4_K_M: prefill 97.32 / decode 13.69 tok/s (6 experts) vs llama.cpp 10.31/4.51 → **27.9× prefill, 3× decode**. v0.3 BF16: **prefill up to 286.55 tok/s** (6 experts, 2K prompt), decode up to 3.03×. RAM needs: 382 GB (Q4 single socket) / 644 GB (BF16 v0.3). AMX kernel: **21.3 TFLOPS/socket sustained, 3.9× vs PyTorch native**; NUMA-aware TP +63% decode on dual socket; expert deferral +1.45× with <0.5% drift (quality-affecting). Community: **2.7–5.1 tok/s decode** on dual-Xeon desktops ([level1techs](https://forum.level1techs.com/t/trying-hybrid-inference-gpu-cpu-with-200-400b-models/247625)). Current [leaderboard](https://ktransformers.net/en/benchmarks) (decode TPS only): 1×5090 + 2×EPYC 9355: Qwen3.5-35B-A3B FP8 **86.2 t/s**, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B **47.3 t/s**; 1×4090 + EPYC AVX2: 19–20 t/s.
- **Infra.** Intel AMX (Xeon Sapphire Rapids+), FlashInfer, multi-hundred-GB RAM, NUMA.
- **Verdict.**
  - **ALREADY-HAVE** — the architecture itself: GPU attention + RAM-resident routed experts + VRAM hot-expert tier is exactly our Engine A shape; our tier is dynamic (LRU + coverage quota) where KTransformers' is static `--kt-num-gpu-experts`. Continuous-CUDA-graph hybrid capture is our queued conditional-CUDA-graphs item — this paper is the evidence it is worth doing (20%→0 launch overhead).
  - **DEAD** — AMX (Zen5 has none; Intel-only), NUMA duplication (single socket), "selective expert activation" 6-vs-8-experts (reduces top-k — the falsified gating class), expert deferral (same class).
  - **Reference points, not transferable**: their prefill 286 tok/s on 2×32C AMX Xeons is the number to compare our prefill lane against on *per-DRAM-byte* basis, not absolute.

## 2) PowerInfer / PowerInfer-2 (SJTU IPADS)

- **Mechanism.** Dense-LLM era: neuron activations follow power law; offline profiler + ILP places **hot neurons GPU-resident, cold in RAM**; adaptive MLP predictors (~7% of params) predict per-input activations; custom neuron-granularity operators; CPU computes cold neurons for batch<32 because PCIe transfer of weights costs more ([arXiv:2312.12456 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.12456v2)).
- **Measured.** RTX 4090 + i9-13900K + 192 GB: FP16 avg **8.32 tok/s (peak 16.06), up to 11.69× vs llama.cpp** (Falcon-40B ReLU); INT4 avg 13.2 tok/s; OPT-30B reaches **82% of an A100's** rate. Critical boundary: **SwiGLU models gain only 1.47–1.7×** (sparsity 40–53% vs ReLU 95%+). No MoE results at all. PowerInfer-2 ([arXiv:2406.06282](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06282)): neuron-cluster decomposition + pipeline overlap + segmented neuron cache; **11.68 tok/s for a 47B MoE on a smartphone**; up to 27.8×.
- **Infra.** x86 AVX2 + one consumer GPU; profiler+ILP offline pass.
- **Verdict.** **DEAD** for our box. (1) Hot-neuron locality is input-statistical; our routed-expert locality *is routing*, already exploited by the VRAM tier at the correct (expert) granularity. (2) SwiGLU sparsity is exactly our falsified gating class. (3) PowerInfer-2's pipeline overlap = our queued micro-group expert pipelining (ALREADY, queued). One durable fact worth keeping: their CPU-vs-transfer cost model (compute on CPU beats moving weights for small batches) is the same economics our --pcie-frac exploits.

## 3) MoE-Infinity (OSDI'24)

- **Mechanism.** Dense parts + KV always GPU-resident; **only routed experts offloaded**. Per-forward Expert Activation Matrix (iEAM, L×E token counts) accumulated per request (rEAM); collection (EAMC, ~120 entries, 3% of requests, cosine-matched in 21–226 µs) predicts future layers' expert sets; prediction weighted by **layer proximity** (1 − (i−l)/L) for both eviction priority (score = predicted prob × (1 − layer_idx/L) ÷ layer tokens — early layers get higher cache priority) and prefetch of next-layer experts during GPU compute. **Explicit negative result: inter-group Markov transitions are statistically unpredictable** (transition probs mostly <0.12, max ~0.3) — they abandoned learned/Markov prefetch for trace matching ([arXiv:2401.14361 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.14361)).
- **Measured.** RTX A5000 24GB + PCIe4, 32–256 GB RAM depending on model. DeepSeek-V2-Lite TPOT **155/169 ms avg/tail vs vLLM 485/493, DeepSpeed 737/803, Ollama 2590** → **3.1–16.7×**; Mixtral-8x7B 120 GB on one GPU; Arctic 900 GB single-GPU feasible. No tok/s reported. One pinned-memory DMA thread per GPU saturates PCIe 4.0.
- **Infra.** PyTorch stack; multi-GPU via NUMA-aware hash placement.
- **Verdict.** **TRANSFERS** — the strongest single transfer of the sweep, with two conditions: (1) the layer-proximity-weighted prefetch of *near-future-layer* experts into the VRAM tier during attention compute attacks exactly our serial attn→moe wall — it moves the same bytes (DRAM→GPU either way) but shifts them into the DRAM-idle attention window; total bytes unchanged, so it does not violate the ceiling wall, it recovers the serialization gap; (2) their trace-history machinery (EAMC) is NOT the transferable part — across-request reuse was found uniform, and our falsified DS4_AHEAD already proved history-guess prefetch can cost more than it saves in the RAM-copy config. The cheap, honest version for Engine A: use the *current request's own recent tokens'* routing at layer i+1 (router logits exist per token; token-level routing autocorrelation) plus proximity decay; price it with the RFC break-even rule in §4 first. **ALREADY-HAVE**: dense+KV always-GPU residency, single DMA thread saturating PCIe, expert-granularity cache.

## 4) llama.cpp --n-cpu-moe + expert-cache RFC + ik_llama.cpp

- **Mechanism.** `--n-cpu-moe N` / `--cpu-moe` (mid-2025): static tensor override keeps N layers' expert tensors in RAM, attention on GPU; decode **re-streams every selected expert over PCIe every token** (selective-copy PR [#15346](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/15346)); no VRAM expert cache in mainline. RFC [#28248](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/28248) `--moe-expert-cache N`: persistent N-slot per-tensor pool, LRU eviction, **prefill bypasses the pool** (uses stock selective-copy so it never evicts decode state), expert-ID→slot-ID remap via `ggml_get_rows` — no kernel changes.
- **Measured.** RFC, RTX 4090 24GB, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next UD-Q3_K_XL, 8K ctx, all experts CPU: 11.55→21.21 t/s (**+84%** at 64 slots; +65% @48, +52% @32); real code-gen +34%; prefill unchanged; PPL within error bars. **Break-even rule: cache pays when hit-rate ≥ 1 − PCIe_BW/RAM_BW; flat-routing models (DeepSeek-class) saw <20% gains.** ik_llama.cpp ([discussion #258](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/258), [repo](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp)): TR7965WX + DDR5-6000 (225 GB/s) + A6000, R1-671B UD-Q2_K_XL: run-time repack `-rtr` **pp 69.85→110.79 t/s (+59%), tg 7.35→13.13 t/s (+79%)**; 9950X + 96GB rig: >4 t/s; fused FFN MoE ops (PR 229), R4/R16 row-interleaved repacks, IQK quant family, FlashMLA-3.
- **Infra.** None beyond gguf + one GPU.
- **Verdict.** **ALREADY-HAVE** — our VRAM expert tier is the RFC's proposal generalized (their pool ≈ our LRU slots; their prefill-bypass ≈ our prefill staging path; their rail guard ≈ our coverage quota). Two things worth extracting: (1) the **hit-rate economics rule** — at 12 GB VRAM our slots are the scarce side; the rule gives the admission bar for any prefetch idea (incl. §3) in one line; (2) it **warns about Engine B**: DS-class 4-of-384 routing is the flat-routing case that gained <20% — price the Engine B VRAM tier expectations accordingly. Row-interleaved repack (ik): **DEAD for our decode lane** (we are measured at 93–100% of the ceiling; repack cannot beat the wall) but a candidate for **prefill** (compute-bound region) — note against the REAP re-bake queue as the same class of intervention.

## 5) Fiddler (SIGCOMM'24)

- **Mechanism.** Gate output arrives early on GPU → predicts which experts each token needs; popular experts (offline-calibrated) pinned in GPU; at runtime **per-expert decision rule**: run on GPU iff `cpu_lat(s) ≤ gpu_lat(s) + trans_lat()`, else **move activations to CPU** and compute there (weights >300 MB/expert at FP16; activation copy <1% latency). Custom AVX512_BF16 CPU kernel ([arXiv:2402.07033v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.07033v3)).
- **Measured.** Quadro RTX 6000 24GB (PCIe3, 56/256 experts GPU-resident) + Xeon Gold 6126; RTX 6000 Ada 48GB (125/256) + Xeon 8480+. Avg **1.26× vs llama.cpp** (best baseline) single-batch decode; 1.56× on LMSYS-Chat-1M; 11.57× beam search; TTFT 1.07–1.65×; Phi-3.5-MoE 6.5× vs DeepSpeed-MII. Popularity placement adds only **+3–5 pts GPU hit rate** over random (25.2% vs 21.9%).
- **Infra.** PyTorch + AVX512-BF16 CPU; needs RAM ≥ expert weights.
- **Verdict.** Mostly **ALREADY-HAVE**: our --pcie-frac 0.35 dma is a static-share version of their per-expert CPU/GPU compute rule; our coverage-quota tier is a dynamic popularity placement (their +3–5 pts over random says popularity placement is low-leverage — consistent with our tier already capturing the skew). **TRANSFERS (small, optional)**: replace the fixed pcie-frac share with their cost-model gate per expert-batch (cpu lanes vs GPU+PCIe lanes per expert, using measured per-expert sizes) — expected gain small over an already-tuned constant; only worth it if we observe load-dependent variance in which experts should go over PCIe. AVX512_BF16 kernel: **DEAD for decode** (no bytes saved) and our CPU-side BF16 GEMM prefill path doesn't exist (weights are IQ3_S).

## 6) AdapMoE / HybriMoE (PKU, ICCAD'24 / DAC'25)

- **Mechanism.** AdapMoE ([arXiv:2408.10284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10284), [repo](https://github.com/PKU-SEC-Lab/AdapMoE)): sensitivity(Hessian)-based adaptive gating (fewer experts per layer), cross-layer prefetch via activation similarity, DP-formulated per-layer cache allocation. HybriMoE ([arXiv:2504.05897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05897), [repo](https://github.com/PKU-SEC-Lab/HybriMoE)): **dynamic intra-layer CPU-GPU split of expert batch** (balance workloads across devices instead of fixed expert→device map), impact-driven inter-layer prefetch, score-based caching; built on KTransformers.
- **Measured.** AdapMoE: −25% expert activations, ~1.75× less prefetch data, ~25% speedup (reported; search-level, not fetched). HybriMoE: **+1.33× prefill, +1.70× decode vs the KTransformers baseline** (abstract, fetched).
- **Infra.** Edge GPU+CPU; Mixtral 8x7B/8x22B.
- **Verdict.** Gating: **DEAD** (falsified class, quality-affecting). Intra-layer CPU-GPU expert-batch split: **ALREADY-HAVE** (that is what --pcie-frac dma is). Inter-layer impact-driven prefetch: same lesson as §3 — merge into one mechanism. DP cache allocation across layers: our five-tier economy with coverage quota already owns capacity arbitration; DEAD as duplicate.

## 7) ExpertFlow, SiDA-MoE

- **ExpertFlow** ([arXiv:2410.17954](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17954v2), fetched): learned T5-style routing predictor (7.21 MB, >90% accuracy) → Expert Cache Engine prefetch + cache-aware token scheduling (Hamming-distance clustering re-batches tokens across two batches); LRU beaten by 15–36 pts, up to **61.15% hit**; correction swaps overlapped with compute. Measured: **up to 10× vs offloading baselines, up to 93.72% VRAM reduction**; A40 48GB + Xeon 6338; Switch/Mixtral/Qwen1.5-MoE/DS-MoE. Verdict: predictor **DEAD** (we have the real router logits — strictly better than any learned predictor; and SpecMD §9 shows prediction accuracy ≠ cache performance anyway); dual-batch overlap = queued micro-group pipelining (ALREADY, queued).
- **SiDA-MoE** (MLSys'24, [PDF](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2024/file/698cfaf72a208aef2e78bcac55b74328-Paper-Conference.pdf), [project page](https://zhixudu.github.io/projects/sida-moe), fetched): 2-layer LSTM+SparseMax **replaces the router**; producer thread predicts next batch's expert table while inference runs; FIFO eviction. Measured: 2.60–3.93× on Switch-base-128/256, but **1.01–1.04× on base-8** and C4 ppl 6.68→18.49 (base-8) — accuracy damage from routing replacement. A100 80GB. Verdict: **DEAD** — router replacement costs quality, Switch-era, hardware mismatch. Producer-consumer overlap = our staging buffers (ALREADY-HAVE).

## 8) HexGen / HexGen-2 / HexGen-3 (Relaxed-System-Lab)

- **Mechanism.** Distributed serving over *heterogeneous GPU fleets*: HexGen — asymmetric TP/PP groups + request coordinator ([repo](https://github.com/Relaxed-System-Lab/HexGen), fetched; ICML'24-era, 2.3× lower latency, 4× peak request rate vs homogeneous-budget baseline, Llama-2); HexGen-2 ([arXiv:2502.07903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07903), fetched): disaggregated prefill/decode across heterogeneous GPUs, scheduling as constraint optimization (graph partitioning + max-flow) over memory capacity and network; **2.0× / avg 1.3× throughput, 1.5× latency, or −30% price budget**, OPT-30B/Llama-70B; HexGen-3 continues autoscaling.
- **Verdict.** **DEAD** for a single box — the entire optimization variable is inter-GPU allocation; we have one GPU. Residual takeaway only: "memory-capacity-aware phase placement" is the same prefill/decode resource asymmetry our engine already exploits (prefill streams experts; decode is DRAM-bound).

## 9) vLLM / SGLang / TensorRT-LLM MoE kernels — what transfers to sm_120

- **vLLM fused MoE** ([design doc](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/design/fused_moe_modular_kernel), fetched): modular kernel chains gate/up GEMM → `act_and_mul` → down GEMM **without intermediate allocations**; per-(E, dtype, M) autotuned Triton shapes; gate+up live as one interleaved `w13` tensor (one launch, two GEMMs in flight). [PyTorch blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/accelerating-moe-model) walks the original Mixtral kernel optimization.
- **sm_120 status**: Machete is **sm_90-only** (Hopper TMA/MMA path — [Red Hat](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2024/10/14/introducing-machete-mixed-input-gemm-kernel)) — **no sm_120 port found**; on Blackwell consumer vLLM falls back to **Marlin W4A16** (works, slower than native; best-tested quant path per [sm120 software blog](https://eurokommerz.at/blog/blackwell-rtx-sm120-software-support)) and Triton fused-MoE; NVFP4 MoE broken on SM120 → Marlin-MoE workaround ([#35566](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/35566), [#47749](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/47749), [#51525](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/51525)); vLLM "Humming" quant kernels now cover SM120/121 ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-10-21-humming), surfaced not deep-read). Pure-Triton fused MoE dispatch reaches 89–131% of CUDA at small batches ([arXiv:2605.23911](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23911v1)) — evidence the fused-dispatch design, not raw Triton, is the transferable part.
- **SGLang**: shared-expert fusion exists as a toggleable feature (`--disable-shared-experts-fusion` in the [KTransformers integration blog](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2025-10-22-KTransformers)) — i.e., shared expert computed inside the routed-expert kernel group.
- **TensorRT-LLM**: MoE-as-dense-GEMM (NVFP4) is **SM100/103-only, up to 1.12×** ([tech blog](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/1.3.0rc15/blogs/tech_blog/blog24_MoE_as_Dense_GEMM.html)); consumer sm_120 served by TensorRT-*RTX*, community reports friction with big MoE ([r/LocalLLaMA](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1mqh2e1/need_help_with_nvidia_tooling_to_run_gptoss_20b)).
- **Verdict.** **TRANSFERS (small, GPU-side)**: (1) **gate+up interleaved single-tensor packing** — if Engine A/B GPU-side MoE kernels treat gate and up as separate tensors, packing them interleaved (vLLM `w13` style) halves launches and pairs the two weight streams; zero DRAM-bytes change on the CPU lane, pure kernel-count/launch win — most relevant for Engine B's 3-sync/zero-graph structure; (2) **shared-expert fusion** into the routed kernel group (SGLang-style) — check both engines; likely partially present. **ALREADY-HAVE**: fused act_and_mul and no-intermediate chaining are ggml-native in the graph. **DEAD**: Machete (sm_90), TRT-LLM DENSEGEMM (SM100/103), Marlin/Machete for IQ3_S (ggml CUDA path owns our formats).

## 10) CPU-side quantized GEMV micro-optimization — "every byte crosses DDR once"

- **Zen5 facts.** First AMD core with **physical 512-bit datapaths** (not double-pumped): 2×512-bit FMA + 2×512-bit loads per cycle, 4×512-bit ops/clock ([NumberWorld teardown](https://www.numberworld.org/blogs/2024_8_7_zen5_avx512_teardown), [Chips and Cheese](https://chipsandcheese.com/p/zen-5s-avx-512-frequency-behavior)); some clock reduction under full AVX-512 load. ISA: AVX512_VNNI (Zen4+), **AVX_VNNI (new in Zen5)**, GFNI (Zen4+), VP2INTERSECT (new), PREFETCHI (new — *instruction* prefetch, not data) ([Phoronix znver5](https://www.phoronix.com/news/AMD-Zen-5-Znver-5-GCC), [Hot Chips 2024](https://hc2024.hotchips.org/assets/program/conference/day2/24_HC2024.AMD.Cohen.Subramony.final.pdf)). No AMX. Desktop dual-channel DDR5 is the wall: full 512-bit FMA across 16C out-runs the IMC — which is precisely our measured regime.
- **Non-temporal loads: DEAD.** `movntdqa` buys nothing from normal write-back memory in userspace — NT load semantics only apply to WC mappings ([vgatherps analysis](http://vgatherps.github.io/2018-09-02-nontemporal/), fetched; [StackOverflow benchmarks](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/40096894/do-current-x86-architectures-support-non-temporal-loads-from-normal-memory)). NT stores are irrelevant (our lane is read-only). Weight streaming that is read-once gains nothing from NT hints; the cache-pollution cost is already nil for streaming rows, and the prefetcher "does a fine job" (same source).
- **Software prefetch distance: mostly DEAD; multi-stride is the live variant.** ggml dot kernels read rows sequentially → hardware prefetcher-optimal; software prefetch adds cost without bandwidth ([arXiv:2412.16001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16001), fetched: multi-strided access patterns raise prefetcher utilization without software prefetch; **up to 12.55× vs Polly, 2.99× vs MKL, 1.98× vs OpenBLAS** on memory-bound kernels). The transferable form: unroll/interleave the IQ3_S dot to keep **multiple concurrent row streams** alive (more cache lines in flight → MSHR-level parallelism), not prefetch intrinsics. Our lane measures 93–100% of ceiling: this targets the 93% tail (low thread counts, CCD imbalance) — bounded gain ≤7%.
- **VNNI vs IQ3_S: DEAD for our format.** `ggml_vec_dot_iq3_s_q8_K` is **2 KB-LUT-based** (sign-magnitude grid `±(1,3,9,27)` via `values_iq3_s` table, consumed with `maddubs`), introduced in [PR #5676](https://app.semanticdiff.com/gh/ggerganov/llama.cpp/pull/5676/overview) — 3-bit packed indices cannot feed VNNI without unpacking, and VNNI paths pay off only for 4/8-bit linear formats (Q2_0 via VNNI: **3.0–3.6×** [kernel PR](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1vhz989/a_llamacpp_pr_makes_q2_0_3036x_faster_on_x86_cpus), [#4301](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/4301)). Since compute is spare (memory-bound at ceiling), the only VNNI route is a re-bake to a VNNI-friendly ≥4.5 bpw format — more bytes, slower lane. Confirms REAP's byte-reduction class (fewer bytes) as the correct axis; VNNI is the wrong axis.
- **State of the art for "every byte crosses DDR once":** saturating aligned streaming loads + hardware prefetch + row-interleaved repack to keep multi-stride streams alive (ik_llama R4/R16; arXiv:2412.16001) — exactly the regime we already sit in (93–100% of ceiling). The remaining % is scheduling/affinity shape, not instruction selection.

## 11) SpecMD — comprehensive cache-policy benchmark (Apple-line, arXiv 2602.03921)

- **Mechanism.** Standardized 4-axis policy space (routing / prefetch / eviction / miss-handling) across OLMoE, Mixtral, Qwen1.5-MoE, Phi-3.5-MoE on A100 80GB with ~5 GB/s emulated expert-store bandwidth, 1–25% cache capacities ([arXiv:2602.03921](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03921v1), fetched).
- **Measured.** **LRU/LFU fail** (expert access is layer-ordered, not temporal): at 1% capacity LRU's collision misses are up to **85× higher** than "Least-Stale" (protect current-pass experts, evict stale ones); Least-Stale alone: 10.7–34.7% TTFT cut (avg ~17%), 1.6–1.9% collision at 5% capacity. **Prediction accuracy ≠ cache performance** (score-based prefetch beats top-k on hit rate). Substitution miss-handling fails everywhere.
- **Verdict.** **TRANSFERS (policy-only, cheap)**: audit our VRAM-tier eviction against Least-Stale semantics — our five-tier economy with coverage quota may already encode "protect current-pass"; if eviction can slot an expert needed *this pass* out under a hot-but-stale tenant, that is the collision class SpecMD prices at up to 85×. One afternoon of ledger reading + a counter; no bytes moved.

## Priced for our box

Ranked by expected effect on the two walls (DRAM ceiling; serial attn→moe chain). Bytes-moved is the currency: a mechanism that moves the same bytes earlier is free; one that moves more bytes is dead on arrival.

1. **Router-informed cross-layer prefetch with layer-proximity decay into the VRAM tier** (MoE-Infinity §3 + HybriMoE §6). Attacks the serial attn→moe chain: same bytes, shifted into the DRAM-idle attention window.
